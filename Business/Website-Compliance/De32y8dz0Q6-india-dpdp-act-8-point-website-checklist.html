    India DPDP Act Website Compliance: The 8-Point Checklist to Avoid Up to ₹250 Crore Penalties

    Author: @ecolatur Source: Instagram Reel De32y8dz0Q6 Extracted: 2026-10-27

    A comprehensive legal and technical audit checklist for Indian business websites under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (DPDP Act). With rules notified in November 2025 and strict statutory enforcement taking effect on 13 May 2027, collecting customer contact forms or deploying ad tracking pixels subjects businesses to penalties up to ₹250 crore. Details the 8 mandatory on-site compliance obligations, including explicit opt-in consent banners, 1-click consent withdrawal, public Grievance Officer disclosures, and mandatory data breach notification protocols.

    The 8 Mandatory Website Compliance Requirements

    1. 01. Plain-Language Privacy Policy: Must clearly and unambiguously disclose: (a) exact categories of personal data collected, (b) specific processing purposes, (c) data retention periods, and (d) third-party data processors or marketing platforms with whom data is shared.
    2. 02. Explicit Opt-In Consent & Equal-Effort Withdrawal: Pre-ticked opt-in checkboxes on lead forms or checkout flows are strictly illegal under DPDP rules. Furthermore, withdrawing consent must be as effortless as granting it: if consent was provided via a single click, withdrawal must also execute via a single click.
    3. 03. Dedicated Grievance Officer Publication: The name, designation, official email, and phone contact of an appointed Grievance Officer must be clearly visible in website footers and contact pages. Data subject requests (e.g., right to data erasure/deletion) must be acknowledged and resolved within statutory time limits.
    4. 04. Transparent Cookie & Tracking Consent: Websites utilizing Meta Pixels, Google Analytics, or third-party ad scripts must inform users upon landing with a clear cookie consent banner providing granular controls (Accept, Reject, Choose).
    5. 05. Written Terms & Conditions: Defines legal terms of service, intellectual property rights, acceptable use policies, and dispute resolution jurisdiction to mitigate commercial liability.
    6. 06. E-Commerce Disclosures (Return, Refund & Shipping): Stores selling goods or digital services online must prominently display: clear refund and cancellation terms, shipping timelines, registered corporate entity name, and physical office address.
    7. 07. Verifiable Parental Consent for Minors: Collecting personal data from individuals under 18 years of age requires verifiable parental consent before account creation or processing.
    8. 08. End-to-End Data Security & Breach Reporting: Mandatory SSL/TLS encryption across all routes. In the event of a personal data breach or leak, companies are legally mandated to report the incident to both affected users and the Data Protection Board of India.

    Regulatory Statutes & Standards

    • Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (Act No. 22 of 2023): Primary Indian legislative framework governing the processing of digital personal data.
    • DPDP Rules, 2025: Subordinate regulatory standards detailing parental consent mechanisms, breach disclosure deadlines, and grievance officer appointment thresholds.
    • Enforcement Target Date: 13 May 2027 (Transition compliance grace period ends).
    • Data Protection Board of India (DPBI): Adjudicatory body responsible for investigating personal data breaches and levying statutory fines.

    Try This

    1. Audit all lead forms on your website to ensure zero checkboxes are pre-selected by default; test submitting a form and confirm consent logs store timestamps and IP records.
    2. Add a dedicated "Grievance Redressal" block in your global website footer listing a named Grievance Officer, email address, physical address, and a maximum 30-day resolution timeline.
    3. Inspect your tracking tags (Meta Pixel, Google Tag Manager); deploy a cookie consent banner that blocks non-essential marketing cookies until the visitor explicitly clicks "Accept".
