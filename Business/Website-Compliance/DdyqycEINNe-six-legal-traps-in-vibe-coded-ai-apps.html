    6 Legal & Compliance Traps in Vibe-Coded AI Apps: The "Per-Violation" Statutory Risk Matrix

    Executive Summary

    AI vibe-coding tools (Cursor, Claude Code, Lovable, v0) allow solo developers to build and launch production software in hours. However, standard LLM prompts produce code optimized solely for visual completeness—completely omitting essential statutory compliance safeguards.

    As startup legal educator Adilet reveals, digital regulatory penalties in the United States and European Union do not scale as a percentage of company revenue. Instead, privacy, copyright, consumer protection, and wiretapping laws mandate per-visitor, per-session, and per-email statutory damages. A vibe-coded app with $0 in revenue can accumulate over $100,000 in legally enforceable liabilities before processing its first customer transaction.

    The 6 Traps & Technical Remediation Matrix

    Trap & Law Vibe-Coding Failure Mode Statutory Penalty Production Technical Fix
    1. COPPA Age Gate
    15 U.S.C. §§ 6501–6506    		 Sign-up modal only collects Email and Password, never validating user age or date of birth. Up to $53,000 per child under 13 years old. Implement mandatory birthdate verification or age-attestation checkbox (I confirm I am 13 years or older) on signup schema.
    2. Google Fonts IP Leak
    EU GDPR / Munich Court Precedent    		 HTML <link> requests dynamic fonts directly from fonts.googleapis.com, exposing EU visitor IP addresses without prior consent. Statutory damages & cease-and-desist claims (~€100 to €2,700 per visitor). Self-host all font files locally (e.g. using next/font/google in Next.js which automatically bundles fonts at build time, zero external network requests).
    3. Session Replay Wiretapping
    California CIPA / Cal. Penal Code § 631    		 Integrating Hotjar, PostHog, or FullStory with default configurations that record user keystrokes in form fields without consent. $5,000 per session under California wiretapping private right of action. Enable strict input masking (data-mask, maskAllInputs: true) and disable session recording until the user accepts the analytics cookie banner.
    4. Waitlist CAN-SPAM Breach
    15 U.S.C. §§ 7701–7713    		 Sending a blast announcement ("We're live!") to waitlist signups without an unsubscribe link or a registered physical address. Up to $53,000 per recipient email. Configure transactional email providers (Resend, Postmark) with footer templates including a 1-click unsubscribe link and a valid commercial postal address.
    5. California Auto-Renewal Law (ARL)
    Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code §§ 17600–17606    		 Stripe Checkout button does not display recurring renewal frequency, billing date, and cancellation instructions immediately adjacent to the pay button. All recurring revenue is legally classified as an "unconditional gift", requiring 100% full refund. Place explicit text immediately above the checkout button: "You will be billed $X/month automatically until cancelled. Cancel anytime via Settings > Billing."
    6. DMCA Safe Harbor Void
    17 U.S.C. § 512(c)    		 Allowing users to upload profile avatars, banners, or images without filing a Designated Copyright Agent with the US Copyright Office. Up to $150,000 per willful copyright infringement by any user. Pay the official $6 filing fee at copyright.gov/dmca-directory to register an agent, and link a DMCA Takedown Notice page in the footer.

    The Hardening Prompt for Coding Agents

    Before deploying any AI-generated frontend or authentication flow, provide this operational prompt to Claude Code, Cursor, or your build agent: 

    Audit this repository against the 6 critical launch compliance vectors:
1. Ensure self-hosted typography: replace all external font links with local bundle imports.
2. Verify signup schema includes strict age verification (COPPA compliance).
3. Audit analytics scripts: mask all input forms and gate tracking behind cookie consent.
4. Ensure all email dispatch templates contain 1-click unsubscribe headers and physical postal footers.
5. In the billing/checkout view, add explicit California ARL recurring terms adjacent to the payment CTA.
6. Verify footer links include Terms of Service, Privacy Policy, and DMCA Agent contact info.
