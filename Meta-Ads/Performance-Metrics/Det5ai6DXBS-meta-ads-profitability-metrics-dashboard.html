    6 Profitability Metrics That Actually Matter in Meta Ads

    Reel by @adsbysambhav 16 sEnglishshared 26 Oct 2026

    Library / Meta-Ads / Performance-Metrics

    A performance marketing guide distinguishing dashboard vanity signals from true business profitability metrics. Explains why advertisers should move their primary decision-making away from platform-reported blended ROAS and raw impression volume toward bottom-line unit economics: aMER (Acquisition Marketing Efficiency Ratio), POAS (Profit on Ad Spend), CPR (Cost Per Result), Contribution Margin, LTV:CAC ratio, and nCAC (New Customer Acquisition Cost).

    Steps

    1. Optimize for POAS (Profit on Ad Spend) instead of Blended ROAS: ROAS only measures top-line GMV and ignores product COGS, packaging, and processing fees. POAS calculates gross profit generated per ad dollar spent, ensuring campaigns are cashflow positive. (00:03)
    2. Track aMER (Acquisition Marketing Efficiency Ratio): Evaluate total new customer revenue divided by total ad expenditure across all channels to assess true acquisition incrementality. (00:02)
    3. Isolate nCAC (New Customer Acquisition Cost): Separate blended CAC (which includes returning customers who would have purchased organically) from first-time acquisition costs to prevent false confidence in ad performance. (00:14)
    4. Monitor Contribution Margin (CM): Calculate the net cash remaining after all variable costs (ad spend, payment gateway cut, fulfillment, and product cost) to confirm that scaling ad spend increases net enterprise bank balances. (00:07)
    5. Benchmark LTV to CAC Ratio: Monitor customer cohort value over 60, 90, and 180 days relative to acquisition cost to ensure payback periods support sustained marketing reinvestment (ideal target > 3:1). (00:09)
    6. Anchor on CPR (Cost Per Result) over CPMs/Impressions: Do not optimize campaigns based on cheap impression counts or CPM fluctuations; base bids and creative iteration strictly on the final conversion action (cost per purchase or cost per qualified lead). (00:05, 00:12)
